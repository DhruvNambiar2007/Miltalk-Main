What is Miltalk?

Miltalk is a handheld AI learning kit that children teach using their own examples. The Vision Kit has a camera for pictures. The Audio Kit has a microphone for sounds. Children choose two categories, collect examples and teach the kit to tell them apart.

Why we started Miltalk

Children encounter AI in their apps, classrooms and everyday lives. We started Miltalk to help them understand how a machine learns from data. The concept we teach is called classification: predicting which category something belongs to. Children explore it by teaching a kit with their own examples and checking its answers.

Why teach classification?

It gives children a clear starting point for understanding AI: they can choose the training data, see the predictions and investigate mistakes. The same task appears in familiar tools, from spam filters to image recognition.

Where you see classification

Recognising a category is useful in many familiar AI features. Miltalk lets children explore that idea with a model they train themselves.

Miltalk Audio Kit

How it works

Children teach classification by doing it. For example, they label pictures as recyclable or non-recyclable, then show the kit a new object. If its prediction is wrong, they look at the training examples, add better ones and test again.

  1. 01

    Train

    Choose your categories

    Show the kit labelled examples of two groups.
  2. 02

    Test

    Try something new

    See which category the kit predicts for an unfamiliar example.
  3. 03

    Investigate

    Find the clue

    Compare the wrong prediction with the examples you gave it.
  4. 04

    Improve

    Change the examples

    Add more useful data, then see what happens on the next test.

What kids learn

01

Better examples. Better predictions.

Children see how the examples they choose shape a model’s answers. Narrow training data can lead to mistakes on something new.

02

Put an idea to the test.

Students make a prediction, change one thing and see whether the evidence supports it.

03

Check the answer behind the score.

A score can be high and still be wrong. Students learn to check each prediction against what actually happened.
